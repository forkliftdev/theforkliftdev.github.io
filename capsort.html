How it works

Two gestures. Nothing else to learn.

Long-press and drag to sort fast. Double-tap when a screenshot needs work first. Both end at the same bucket.

↳

Screenshots only

CapSort reads Apple's screenshots collection, never your full camera roll. It's not a general photo organizer, and that's the point.

✓

Verified Export Preview

iOS crop has leaked uncropped originals into Slack for years. Before anything leaves your phone, CapSort shows you the final image and confirms the crop actually committed.

◎

Metadata scrubbing you can see

A mini map shows the exact GPS pin buried in a screenshot's metadata, then strips it, along with timestamp and device info, on export.

◆

Streaks, not spreadsheets

Daily goals, milestone celebrations, and a real backlog-to-zero projection, all from what you've actually sorted, not an algorithm's guess.

⌗

Your own buckets

Name your own folders and drag straight into them. No auto-categorization to second-guess, no AI deciding what a screenshot "is."

⏳

One trash dialog, not one per photo

Deletions queue locally during a sort session. One confirmation empties the whole batch, instead of interrupting you after every swipe.

Why it exists

Not another AI cleanup tool.

The problem isn't finding a screenshot, it's the pile. Receipts, recipes, code snippets, and inspiration all stack up with no plan to deal with them. Most people's answer is to ignore it until the storage warning shows up.

CapSort skips the AI-organizer pitch on purpose. Auto-categorization means second-guessing a model's judgment call on every photo. A long-press-and-drag gesture means you decide, once, per screenshot, and move on. Most backlogs clear in minutes.

The trust problem is the sharper one: iOS's crop tool has shipped uncropped originals to Slack, clipboard, and shares by accident, multiple iOS versions running. CapSort's Verified Export Preview is the screen Apple has never built, because building it would mean admitting the OS needs it.
